Privacy policy · draft

Draft · November 2026

Only the bump leaves the phone.

Asphalt records road defects, not people. This page lists every field a report contains, what is never collected, and how reports are used.

This draft is written from the published event schema. It has not yet had a legal review.

01Summary

The short version.

  • Asphalt sends one small report when a phone feels a road defect. Nothing is sent between defects.
  • A report has no name, account, phone number, device ID or advertising ID.
  • Raw sensor readings stay on the phone. A report carries six summary numbers instead.
  • Location is recorded only at the moment of a defect. Routes and trip histories are never uploaded.
  • The code is open source under Apache 2.0, so anyone can check these statements.

03What is never collected

Not in any report.

  • Names or user accounts
  • Email or phone numbers
  • Device IDs or IMEI
  • Advertising IDs
  • Continuous location
  • Routes or trip history
  • Raw sensor streams
  • Contacts, photos or audio

04When a report is made

Only on the road, only at a defect.

Asphalt runs inside an app that a fleet, partner or city has chosen to ship. That app decides when detection is on.

Detection pauses below about 9 mph. A report is made only when the phone feels an impact strong enough to cross the alert level. Reports are batched and uploaded together.

Apps that include Asphalt must tell their drivers or users that road defect reports are collected, and let them turn detection off.

05How reports are used

To find and rank road defects.

The server groups nearby reports into one defect and scores how certain it is. Scores rise with more reports, more vehicle types and more recent reports.

Defects are shown on maps and returned through the API. Individual reports are not sold.

Defect data from a pilot is not shared with third parties without the pilot partner's written consent.

06Who controls the data

Whoever runs the server.

The Asphalt backend is open source. A city or fleet can run it on its own servers, so reports never leave its systems.

The v1 server code does not write IP addresses into the report store. A hosting provider may still log them.

In a hosted pilot, the city or fleet owns its defect data. Asphalt processes it only to run the pilot.

Public records handling is agreed with each city's records office during pilot setup.

07Retention

How long reports are kept.

Reports older than 90 days stop counting toward a defect's confidence score. Version 1 does not delete them automatically.

Pilot data is deleted on request when a pilot ends.

08This website

What this site loads and stores.

  • This site sets no cookies and runs no analytics. The sample dashboard remembers your last chosen city in your browser only.
  • Fonts load from Google Fonts. The sample dashboard loads map tiles from Esri and its map library from jsDelivr. Each service sees your IP address.
  • The detector demo runs in your browser. If you use your phone's motion sensor, readings stay on the page.
  • If you send the pilot form, we use your details only to reply about a pilot.

Nothing you type into the pilot form is stored by this site.

09Contact

Questions about privacy.

Open an issue on GitHub. Please do not include personal details in a public issue.
