Road-quality intelligence for public works

No new hardware · No personal data · Open source

Find and rank road defects across your city, continuously.

Asphalt turns phones already on your roads into a road-quality survey that never goes stale. No new hardware, no personal data.

Built for city public works teams. It also serves fleet operators, auto insurers and mapping platforms.

01The problem

Pothole work still starts with a phone call.

Most cities find road defects in three ways. Each one is slow, partial or expensive.

Today311 driven

Repairs follow complaints.

Crews fix what residents report. Busy, vocal areas get service. Quiet streets wait until damage is severe.

With Asphalt

Defects are found and ranked whether or not anyone called them in.

TodayStale surveys

The survey is out of date.

A contractor pavement survey is a snapshot. Conditions change long before the next one is funded.

With Asphalt

The picture updates every time an equipped vehicle drives the street.

TodayVerification trips

Crews drive out to check.

Someone has to confirm a report before work is scheduled. That trip costs crew hours and fixes nothing.

With Asphalt

Several independent reports confirm a defect before a truck rolls.

Spend crew hours on repairs, not on finding the work.

05Where the data comes from

Your residents do not need to install anything.

Asphalt is a software kit, not a consumer app. It runs inside apps on Android phones that already travel your streets.

Source A

Your own fleet

Phones mounted in vehicles you already run: sanitation, transit, parking or inspection. You control coverage from day one.

Source B

Partner fleets

Delivery or contractor fleets add Asphalt to an app their drivers already use. Coverage grows without city staff time.

Source C · optional

An existing resident app

A city or partner app can include Asphalt as an opt-in feature. It is never required for a pilot.

Pilot default: your own fleet. Your drivers install an Android app with the Asphalt SDK on their phones, mount them in the vehicle and drive their usual routes. We handle the setup.

Phones need Android. Detection pauses below about 9 mph, so parked and idling vehicles send nothing.

Coverage comes from vehicles already on the road, with no resident sign-ups.

06How it works for a city

From a vehicle on the road to a crew on site.

  1. Install on vehicles

    Add Asphalt to an Android app on phones in your vehicles. No new hardware to buy or wire in.

  2. Detect

    As vehicles drive, each phone feels impacts and tells a pothole from a speed bump. Only the defect is sent.

  3. Confirm through multiple reports

    A defect counts once at least two reports land within about 100 feet. Each extra vehicle raises confidence.

  4. Act and verify

    Crews fix the most certain defects first. Vehicles keep driving past, so you see whether each fix held.

08Trust and data governance

Know exactly what is collected, and what never is.

Each report describes one road defect. It carries no name, account or device ID, and no route.

ONE REPORT · EVERY FIELDPublished schema
What is sentWhy
Defect typePothole, speed bump, rough patch or unknown
Strength, 0 to 1Rank severe defects higher
Location at that moment, with GPS accuracyPlace the defect and discount vague fixes
Vehicle speed and typeCompare car and motorcycle reports fairly
TimeRecent reports count more
Six summary numbersDescribe the impact without raw sensor data
Phone model and Android versionCorrect for differences between phones
Random report and trip IDsRemove duplicates; the trip ID resets each trip

Never collected

  • Names or accounts
  • Device or ad IDs
  • Routes or trip history
  • Raw sensor streams

09Limitations and mitigations

Where the data is weak, and what we do about it.

Version 1 makes deliberate trade-offs. Here is each one, paired with how it is handled today.

RefLimitationMitigationStatus
  1. L/01

    No per-phone tuning

    Alert levels are set by vehicle type, not tuned to each phone.

    A defect needs agreement from several vehicles, so one oddly sensitive phone cannot create it alone.

    Roadmap

    Planned

  2. L/02

    Quiet roads stay uncertain

    Streets with little traffic collect few reports, so confidence stays low.

    The confidence cutoff is adjustable. A pilot can also route fleet vehicles down quiet streets.

    Available in v1

  3. L/03

    Fake locations are limited, not prevented

    A spoofed phone can submit false reports.

    One trip cannot confirm a defect alone. Pilot fleets use known vehicles, not anonymous phones.

    Roadmap

    Planned

  4. L/04

    Tall buildings blur location

    Downtown GPS can drift 100 to 160 feet, wider than the 100-foot grouping radius.

    Every report carries its GPS accuracy. Down-weighting weak fixes is planned, not yet applied in v1.

    Roadmap

    Planned
